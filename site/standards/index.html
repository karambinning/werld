EDITORIAL STANDARDS

How WERLD/ decides what to show, how it labels it, and what happens when it gets something wrong. WERLD/ is not a member of a press regulator. It voluntarily follows the principles of the Editors' Code of Practice, especially on accuracy, privacy, grief, children, suicide and court reporting.

1. Accuracy and attribution

  • Every headline on WERLD/ is the publishing outlet's own wording, credited by name and linked to the original. WERLD/ does not rewrite, summarise or add to headlines.
  • WERLD/ shows the time it first saw each report, which can be later than the time it was published.
  • A story is MULTI-SOURCE when two or more independent outlets report it. Outlets carrying near-identical wording (usually one agency report, syndicated) count as one origin. SINGLE SOURCE means only one origin so far. Neither label means WERLD/ has verified the facts; they tell you how widely an event is being reported.
  • DEVELOPING means new reports arrived in the last three hours.

2. Automation and AI

  • Grouping reports into stories, tagging desks, places and threads, and ranking the Brief are done automatically by Binning.AI's software, using fixed rules, not a generative AI model. The rules are described on How WERLD/ works.
  • No AI model writes text that appears on the WERLD/ website.
  • WERLD/ videos may use an AI-generated voice. When they do, the video says so and is labelled as AI-generated on the platform. Video scripts are checked and approved by a person before they are published, and only say what the cited outlets reported.
  • Automatic grouping can be wrong: two different events can be merged, or a place mis-tagged. When we find this, we fix it and log it.

3. Sensitive stories

  • Stories involving sexual offences, suicide, harm to children, or criminal proceedings in the UK are held back automatically. They appear on WERLD/ only after a person at Binning.AI has checked them, and they are never featured in the Brief, the headline ticker or a video.
  • WERLD/ follows UK reporting restrictions, including the lifelong anonymity of victims of sexual offences and restrictions on identifying children in legal proceedings, and avoids publishing anything that could prejudice active criminal proceedings.
  • Where a story involves suicide, we include details of support: Samaritans, free on 116 123 (UK and Ireland).

4. Sources

  • WERLD/ draws on established news organisations, agencies, specialist outlets, official sources and hazard alerts. It covers English-language reporting only for now.
  • Outlets under UK sanctions (including RT and Sputnik) are not used. Other state-funded outlets are labelled STATE MEDIA where they appear.
  • Sport, entertainment and celebrity news are outside WERLD/'s beat.
  • Outlets that do not want their headlines on WERLD/ can ask us to stop, and we will.

5. Corrections

When WERLD/ gets something wrong (a wrong grouping, a wrong place or label, or a story that should not be there) we correct or remove it as quickly as we can and record what happened in the public corrections log. Errors in an outlet's own reporting are for that outlet to correct; when they do, WERLD/ follows the corrected version.

6. Complaints and takedown requests

  • Email binning.ai.incorporated@gmail.com with the story code (shown on every story page), the page address, and what is wrong. You don't need an account, and your complaint is not published.
  • We acknowledge complaints within 24 hours.
  • Where a complaint concerns possible legal harm (defamation, a reporting restriction, contempt of court, privacy or the safety of a person), we remove the story from WERLD/ while we look at it, normally within a few hours of reading the complaint.
  • We reply with our decision and the reasons. Corrections and removals are recorded in the log; we will not repeat a complainant's personal details there.
  • Requests to remove personal data are handled under UK data protection law; see Privacy.

7. Independence

WERLD/ carries no advertising and no sponsored content, and takes no payment from the organisations it covers or links to. If that changes, we will say so here.
